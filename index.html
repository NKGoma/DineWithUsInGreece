Sunday Evening

◇

Page I — The Invitation

There are places we visit,
and places we imagine ourselves into.

Tonight, it is Greece.

White and blue, sunlight on stone,
the quiet hum of a late summer evening
that stretches just a little longer than expected.

This is not just dinner.
It is a table set somewhere else.

Come dressed for warm air and slow time.
As if, just for tonight, we never had to leave.

II

The First Taste

It begins, as all good evenings do, slowly.

Χωριάτικη σαλάτα

Horiátiki saláta

Greek Village Salad

It arrives without ceremony — sun-ripened tomatoes, crisp cucumber, sharp olives, and a thick slice of feta resting on top, untouched.

Juicy, salty, fresh. Each bite tastes like something real, something uncomplicated — like food that belongs outdoors, under an open sky.

◈

Φέτα με μέλι & θυμάρι

Féta me méli & thymári

Baked Feta with Honey & Thyme

Then, something softer. The feta warmed just enough to give in, drizzled with golden honey that pools at the edges, finished with thyme that lingers gently in the background.

It melts slightly as you cut into it — sweet, salty, delicate.

◈

Χαλούμι με ρόδι

Haloúmi me ródi

Grilled Halloumi with Pomegranate Molasses

And then, a contrast. Halloumi, grilled until golden, its edges crisp, its center still soft. Over it, pomegranate molasses — deep, tangy, almost syrup-like.

The bite is rich, bright, unexpected. The kind of flavor that stays with you for a moment longer than it should.

III

The Table Settles

The main dish does not rush in. It arrives when the table is ready.

Μουσακάς

Mousakás

Moussaka

Layers of tender eggplant, slow-cooked meat, spiced gently, then covered in a thick, creamy béchamel. Baked until the top turns golden, almost caramelized.

It is warm, rich, and deeply comforting — each layer soft, each bite balanced between savory and smooth. A dish that feels like it has taken its time, and asks you to do the same.

✦

The name arrives from Arabic — مسقعة (musaqa‘a), meaning “the chilled one.” The dish exists across Egypt and the Levant in an older form: no béchamel, no oven, often served at room temperature. In Egypt it is layered with fried eggplant and spiced tomato sauce — simpler, perhaps, but no less considered.

The Greek version was reimagined in the 1920s by a chef trained in France, who added the creamy béchamel crown. Two cultures, one dish, different stories. Both worth the table.

◈

Τζατζίκι

Tzatzíki

Cucumber Yogurt Dip

Cool yogurt, finely cut cucumber, garlic, a hint of dill and mint. Light, fresh, and sharp enough to cut through the richness.

A quiet contrast that makes everything else taste clearer.

IV

The Sweet Turn

Somewhere along the evening, things become softer.

Πορτοκαλόπιτα

Portokalópita

Greek Orange Cake

A delicate orange cake, soaked slowly in syrup until it becomes almost impossibly moist. Bright citrus rises first, followed by warmth, sweetness, and a gentle depth that settles in.

Light, but indulgent. Sweet, but never heavy. The kind of dessert that does not end the evening — it extends it.

✦

A small secret inside the name: portokáli (πορτοκάλι) is Greek for orange. In Arabic, it is burtuḳāl (برتقال). In Turkish, portakal. In Persian, portegāl. All the same word, carried across the Mediterranean from Portugal, where Europeans first encountered this fruit.

One fruit. One word. A whole sea of languages.

V

What We Sip

Glasses catch the light.

Μαστίχα λεμόνι

Mastícha lemóni

Mastiha Lemon Twist

Fresh lemon sharp and clean
+
Mastiha subtle, almost pine-like sweetness
+
Mastiha cream smooth, fragrant — spooned on top

It is refreshing, slightly unusual, and somehow exactly right for the moment.

✦

Mastiha is a resin that comes only from the mastic trees of Chios — a Greek island that holds the world’s only supply. For centuries it was traded across the Mediterranean, known to Greeks and Arabs alike as medicine, breath freshener, perfume. The ancient Egyptians used it in embalming. The Ottoman court chewed it. Tonight, it arrives in a glass.

VII

The Last Page

◇

Nothing ends suddenly.

Just slower conversations,
softer laughter,
and the feeling of having been somewhere else for a while.

A table,
a few dishes,
a shared evening —

and somehow,
a whole place created in between.
